# Unipicks — Product Source of Truth

## 1. Product Vision

Unipicks is a **university student companion**.

It is a student-focused platform that helps university students discover better-value products, services, experiences, and opportunities while helping businesses understand and serve the university student market.

The first market is **affordable food**.

The long-term vision can expand into areas such as events, recreation, entertainment, activities, student services, and eventually professional and life-stage offers.

The ambition is much bigger than a food-deal app:

> **Unipicks should become a must-have university app that every high-school graduate in Rwanda knows about and wants to have.**

The platform should grow with its users rather than becoming irrelevant when university ends.

---

## 2. Origin Story

The idea for Unipicks came from a real situation involving a business called **Mr. Chips**.

The business owner wanted to attract Kepler College students but did not know how to reach them effectively.

The user heard the problem through a friend and started thinking:

> How can a local business reach university students and give them a reason to buy?

That question became the starting point for Unipicks.

The idea is therefore rooted in a real market problem:

**Businesses want student customers, while students need affordable products and services.**

Unipicks sits between those two sides.

---

## 3. The Core Problem

### Students

University students can have limited spending power and may depend heavily on parents or other support.

They are often treated as normal customers even though their financial situation can be different from working adults.

Unipicks should make students feel:

> **“Someone actually thought about me as a student.”**

The first problem Unipicks wants to solve is:

> **Help students save money and recognize the difference between a good deal and a better deal.**

A better deal is not necessarily the cheapest deal.

The platform should help students consider:

- Price
- Quality
- Location/convenience
- Discount/value
- Student feedback
- Business reputation

The desired habit is:

> **“If I want something, I should check Unipicks first.”**

---

## 4. Business Problem

Businesses do not only need more customers. They need the **right customers at the right time**.

A business may have:

- Slow days
- Empty capacity
- Unsold products
- New products that need customers
- Demand at certain times
- A desire to reach university students specifically

Unipicks should help businesses reach the student market and make better decisions about what to offer.

The business should feel:

> **“Unipicks connects me to a market segment I can actually serve.”**

Unipicks should help businesses understand:

- What students want
- When they want it
- Where demand exists
- What prices/offers attract students
- Which products perform well
- Whether students are satisfied
- Whether the business is improving

---

## 5. The Economic Idea

A major idea behind Unipicks is **student market segmentation**.

University students can be treated as a distinct customer segment. Businesses can create student-specific prices, bundles, promotions, and deals.

This connects to the idea of **third-degree price discrimination**, where businesses charge different identifiable market segments different prices.

For Unipicks, the practical principle is:

> **A business can offer a special student price when it can confidently identify the customer as part of the student segment.**

This is why student verification matters.

Student-specific pricing should create value for both sides:

**Students:** better affordability.

**Businesses:** access to a distinct market and potential additional demand.

---

## 6. What Makes Unipicks Different

Unipicks should not try to be another general delivery platform.

A general platform can serve everyone.

Unipicks should be **picky**.

The philosophy is:

> **Be selective about what students see because students deserve better choices.**

The platform should focus on:

- University students
- Student-specific value
- Useful deals
- Relevant discovery
- Quality
- Trust
- Social proof
- Demand intelligence

This creates a major difference from a general marketplace:

> **Other platforms serve the general market. Unipicks understands and serves the university market.**

The core student journey begins with discovery:

**Discover → Understand value → Order → Pay → Receive → Rate → Return**

The first version focuses on food deals.

Students should be able to:

- Discover deals
- Compare value
- See price and discount
- See quality/reputation signals
- Search
- Order
- Pay
- Receive a QR/code for redemption
- Rate the deal and service
- Comment
- Communicate with businesses

The experience should be:

- Mobile-first
- Fast
- Visual
- Simple
- Social
- Useful

The platform can borrow useful behaviors from social apps without becoming a copy of them.

## 8. Social Discovery

Unipicks should have a social layer because students naturally make decisions together.

The goal is not to build a social network for its own sake.

Social features should improve:

- Discovery
- Trust
- Group ordering
- Sharing
- Student engagement
- Business quality

Students can have unique usernames.

Students can search for other students by username and send friendship requests.

### Friendship principle

Friendships require **mutual consent**.

A student does not become another student's friend simply because they followed or searched for them.

The basic flow is:

**Student A sends request → Student B accepts → Friendship exists.**

Students should be able to remove or manage friendships later.

Unipicks may learn from real interaction patterns, but it should never create personal relationships without consent.

## 9. Group Orders

Group Orders are a major part of the Unipicks identity.

The idea is:

> **“I found a good deal on Unipicks. Let's order together.”**

Group Orders are designed around real student behavior.

Students may normally move around in pairs or small groups, but during lunch, breaks, or social activities groups can grow to five or more people.

### Group Order benefits

**Easy ordering**

One student starts the group order and shares a link/code.

Other students join and select their own items.

**Independent payment**

Each student normally pays for their own order.

However, a student can choose to buy for one or more other students.

This supports both:

- Independence
- Generosity

**Better group deals**

Businesses can create special group-only offers.

For example:

> 5+ students → special group price

The business can decide the minimum and, when necessary, maximum group size.

**Cheaper delivery**

More students ordering together can make delivery more efficient.

The student should see the potential saving clearly before joining.

Example:

> Normal delivery: 1,500 RWF  
> Group delivery: 700 RWF  
> You save: 800 RWF

The saving should speak for itself.

### Group access

A group order can be shared using a link or code.

Anyone receiving the link can join, but they must have a verified student account.

The group does not require everyone to already be friends.

This creates a natural growth loop:

**One student starts → shares → friends/classmates join → new students create accounts → more people use Unipicks.**

---

## 10. Student Verification

Student verification is a core part of the marketplace.

Businesses offering student-specific prices need confidence that the offer reaches the intended market segment.

A student account should therefore be connected to a verified university education email.

The basic principle is:

> **Verified student status protects the value of student pricing.**

The platform should not ask students to repeatedly verify without reason.

As long as their education email remains valid and verified, their student status can remain active.

The system should monitor the verification state and eventually guide students through the next life stage when their student period appears to be ending.

## 11. Student Lifecycle

Unipicks should grow with the person.

### Stage 1 — Student Unipicks

The university stage focuses on:

- Affordable food
- Student deals
- Group Orders
- Events
- Recreation
- Student experiences
- Social discovery
- Student-specific value

The normal university period is expected to be around four years, but the system should not blindly assume every degree lasts exactly four years.

### Stage 2 — Professional Unipicks

Near the end of university, Unipicks can ask:

> **“Congratulations! You graduated. Continue with Professional Unipicks?”**

Professional Unipicks can focus on early-career needs such as:

- Gym memberships
- Coworking spaces
- Professional services
- Lifestyle offers
- Networking
- Other young-professional opportunities

The initial concept is approximately two years.

### Stage 3 — Future Life Stage

After the professional period, Unipicks should create another stage based on what users need at that point in life.

The exact model is intentionally not decided yet.

The account itself should not simply disappear because a special marketplace stage ends.

## 12. Smart Discovery

Unipicks should not show the same deals to every student.

The platform should learn from behavior and context.

Useful signals can include:

- Day
- Time
- Price
- Deal age
- Expiry
- Location
- Views
- Searches
- Orders
- Repeat purchases
- Ignored deals
- Ratings
- Comments
- Business performance
- Student behavior
- Sponsored status
- Social engagement

### Important ranking principles

**Day and time are strong signals.**

A lunch-time food deal should behave differently from a late-evening offer.

**Price matters strongly.**

A low final price can be more useful than a large percentage discount.

**Expiry matters.**

Deals expiring today may deserve more visibility because they are immediately useful to students and can help businesses avoid wasted inventory.

**Quality matters.**

A cheap deal that consistently receives poor ratings should not automatically win because it is cheap.

**Business performance matters.**

Businesses that consistently provide good experiences should build trust over time.

**Progress matters.**

A business that is improving should have a path to recover from previous poor performance.

---

## 13. Location

Location should support discovery without making the app intrusive.

Unipicks should not necessarily demand precise location immediately when the student opens the app.

At checkout, the platform can ask:

> **Are you on campus or off campus?**

If off campus, location can be requested.

This information can help Unipicks understand:

- Where students buy
- Where demand exists
- Where businesses should operate
- How far students are willing to travel
- How demand changes between campus and home

Location is therefore not only a filter.

It can become part of **demand intelligence**.

The platform should collect only what is needed and clearly explain why location is useful.

---

## 14. Quality, Ratings and Trust

Unipicks should use student feedback to encourage businesses to maintain quality.

The social layer can include:

- Views
- Likes
- Comments
- Ratings
- Business replies

The purpose is not to copy social media.

The purpose is to create accountability:

**Business quality → Student experience → Feedback → Public reputation → Business behavior → Better quality**

### Official ratings

Only students who completed an order can give an official rating.

Students rate:

- Deal quality: 1–5
- Service quality: 1–5

Deal and service ratings are equally important.

A business may have a good product but poor service, or excellent service with a poor product.

Ratings should therefore remain separate.

Rating identity should be private from the business.

Businesses see aggregate performance, not which student gave a particular rating.

Students can also write comments.

Comments are a separate social feature from the numerical rating.

Businesses can reply.

Students can edit and delete their own comments.

Edited comments show an edited label publicly, while edit history remains available to administrators.

Deleted comments disappear publicly but remain available internally for moderation.

---

## 15. Moderation

Students should feel safe expressing honest feedback.

Businesses should have limited ability to hide comments only for legitimate reasons such as:

- Spam
- Abusive/offensive language
- False or misleading information
- Unrelated content
- Personal information
- Duplicate content
- Other justified reasons

A hidden comment should remain in internal records.

Students can report comments or businesses.

Businesses can also report students.

Reports should be handled confidentially.

Serious violations can lead to:

- Warning
- Temporary suspension
- Permanent removal

A suspended business should disappear from the student-facing marketplace while administrators retain its history.

If reinstated, the business can return with a fresh public reputation while historical information remains available to administrators.

## 16. Business Experience

Business onboarding should collect enough information for verification and trust.

Required information can include:

- Business name
- Phone number
- MoMo Pay code
- Location/address
- Business registration information
- Business category
- Logo
- Menu/products

Owner identification can be optional depending on verification requirements.

Administrators should be able to review submitted business information.

### Business posts

For the MVP, a business post should provide clear student value through an offer/deal.

Example:

> **New Student Burger — Buy 1, Get 2. Bring your friends!**

Businesses should have freedom to decide what offer makes sense for their situation.

Examples:

- Slow Tuesday
- New product
- Excess inventory
- Exam period
- Student promotion
- Low-demand hours
- Seasonal offer

The platform should not force every business into the same discount format.

## 17. Business Progress

Businesses should have a simple indication of whether they are improving.

For example:

> **↗ Improving**

The underlying model can consider:

- Deal quality
- Service quality
- Response time
- Fulfillment
- Recent ratings
- Complaints
- Reports
- Deal performance
- Recent improvement

The first version should avoid an unnecessarily complicated score.

The principle is:

> **Money can buy exposure. Good performance earns trust. Progress matters.**

A business should have a way to improve rather than being permanently punished by old mistakes.

## 18. Order and Payment Journey

The intended order flow is:

**Student chooses deal → Places order → Business confirms → Student pays → QR/PIN generated → Business prepares → Student receives order → Business marks redeemed → Student can rate**

Order states should clearly communicate what is happening.

Examples:

- 🟡 Waiting for confirmation
- 🟢 Confirmed — Pay now
- 🔵 Payment processing
- ✅ Paid — Show QR/PIN
- ✅ Ready
- ✅ Redeemed
- ❌ Declined
- ⏰ Expired
- 💰 Refunded

### Business confirmation

Businesses have a limited time to respond.

If they decline, they should provide a reason such as:

- Item unavailable
- Too busy
- Business closed
- Price changed
- Other

The student should receive a useful clarification rather than simply seeing “Declined.”

If the business does not respond within the allowed period, the order can automatically cancel.

### Payment

Students have a limited time to pay after confirmation.

If payment does not happen within that period, the order expires.

After successful payment, the student receives a QR code and short PIN.

The redemption remains valid only until the earliest applicable order/deal expiry.

### Ready status

A business can choose a pickup/service window.

The business should mark the order **Ready** in the app and can also call the student.

The digital Ready status creates useful evidence and data even when real-world communication happens by phone.

## 19. Demand Intelligence

One of Unipicks' biggest long-term advantages is the data created by student demand.

Searches with few or no results are valuable signals.

For example:

> Students repeatedly search for pizza near campus, but there are few suitable offers.

That creates a **demand gap**.

Unipicks should eventually identify:

- High-demand products with low supply
- High-demand locations with few businesses
- Frequently searched unavailable products
- Demand at specific times
- Price opportunities
- Growing categories

Administrators can then approach businesses and say:

> **“Students are looking for this. Would you like to serve them?”**

This connects directly to the original Mr. Chips problem.

### Business intelligence

Aggregated demand intelligence can eventually be offered to business developers and other businesses.

The goal is not to sell individual student identities or private behavior.

The valuable product is aggregated insight:

- What students want
- Where demand exists
- When demand happens
- What price ranges attract interest
- Which categories are underserved
- How demand changes over time

This creates a possible second business model:

**Marketplace revenue + Business Intelligence revenue**

The principle remains:

> **If the business works, the student should benefit.**

Business intelligence should ultimately help create better supply, better offers, and better prices for students.

## 20. Social Growth Loop

Unipicks should create natural reasons for students to bring other students onto the platform.

A strong example is Group Orders:

**Student discovers deal → Starts group order → Shares link → Friends/classmates create verified accounts → Join → Everyone orders → Students save money → More students discover Unipicks**

The platform should not depend on artificial referral pressure.

The product itself should make sharing useful.

The desired reaction is:

> **“Unipicks is our own app. Let's order together.”**

## 21. Monetization

The initial philosophy is:

**Students use Unipicks for free.**

Basic business participation should also be free.

A future paid business tier can provide features such as:

- Verification features
- Follow functionality
- Additional visibility
- Business tools

Paid visibility should not automatically mean a business becomes the number-one result.

The platform should balance:

- Organic discovery
- Personalized sponsored discovery
- Trending/social discovery

The principle is:

> **Money can buy exposure. Good performance earns trust.**

Advertising can be considered later when Unipicks has strong daily traffic.

The priority is to build genuine usage before depending heavily on advertising revenue.

---

## 22. Kepler Launch

Kepler is the first important environment for proving the concept.

Unipicks should be considered successful at the first stage when three things happen together:

1. **Many students register.**
2. **Many businesses/deals join.**
3. **Students regularly save money and return to use Unipicks.**

The third is especially important.

Registrations alone do not prove that the product works.

The real proof is repeated student value combined with business participation.

## 24. Data → Information → Insight → Decision

Unipicks should use Business Analytics as part of its product philosophy.

The goal is not to collect data simply because data exists.

The progression should be:

**Data → Information → Insight → Decision**

Example:

**Data:** 400 searches for pizza.

**Information:** Most searches happen between 12:00 and 14:00 near campus.

**Insight:** Student demand for affordable pizza is concentrated around lunch.

**Decision:** Approach suitable businesses and encourage a student pizza offer.

This is where Unipicks can become valuable beyond transactions.

## 25. Product Principles

### Student first

Student benefit is central.

If businesses become more successful through Unipicks, students should ultimately benefit through better availability, quality, convenience, or prices.

### Picky by design

Unipicks should not try to show everything.

It should help students find what is relevant and valuable.

### Data-driven, not random

Recommendations should learn from real behavior and context.

Random discovery should not replace analysis.

### Social where it creates value

Social features should improve discovery, trust, sharing, or group activity.

Do not add social features simply because other apps have them.

### Consent matters

Friendships require mutual consent.

Personal relationships should never be created automatically.

### Student status protects student value

Student-specific offers depend on credible student verification.

### Performance earns trust

Paid visibility can provide exposure, but good performance should build long-term trust and discovery.

### Progress matters

Businesses should be able to improve.

### Evidence before expansion

New categories and features should be added because demand or evidence supports them.

### Do not build everything at once

The long-term vision is large.

The MVP must remain focused.

**Student discovers → Student sees value → Student buys → Student saves → Business makes a sale → Business learns → Business improves → Student gets better choices → Student returns**

Ten years from now, the goal is for a person in Rwanda to hear:

> **“Unipicks.”**

and immediately think:

> **“That's the university companion app.”**

The ultimate ambition is for every high-school graduate entering university in Rwanda to know about Unipicks and see it as something they should have.

Unipicks should begin with food, but its identity should be much bigger:

> **A platform that grows with people and helps businesses serve the market around them better.**

## 29. Development Rule

This document is the **Product Source of Truth**.

Technical implementation documents may define how the system works, but major product decisions should be checked against this vision.

The development process should remain:

**IDEA → UNDERSTAND → PLAN → IMPLEMENT → TEST → MEASURE → DOCUMENT → IMPROVE**

Do not add features simply because they are technically possible.

Do not rebuild working systems without evidence.

Do not sacrifice student trust for short-term growth.

Do not sacrifice business value for student growth.

Build the marketplace so that:

> **When the business works, the student benefits.**
